##	Introduction

The Performance Standards page displays the status of all performance standards configured for your installation, allowing users to assess the health of the barrier model from a standards compliance perspective rather than by area or by hazard.

Because each BSP deployment is configured to the needs of the facility, the performance standards present on this page, and the names used for them, will vary between installations. However, the underlying categories and the concepts they represent are consistent across all deployments. If the category names in your deployment differ from those described below, the descriptions will still help you understand what each category is concerned with.

![Status Tables](~/assets/bsp/pages/image32.png)

##	Categories

Performance standards are grouped into categories that broadly correspond to the stages of the journey from normal operations to major accident [described in Concepts.](/bsp/user-guide/concepts/performance-standards/) Each category groups together the standards relevant to a particular stage of that journey.

:::note[Please Note:]
 The names of each of these categories may differ from each BSP configuration but the concept will be the same.
:::

#### Prevent initiation

Standards in this category relate to controls that keep the facility within its normal safe operating envelope and prevent a deviation from developing. These are the first line of defence, the barriers that stop a hazardous situation from arising in the first place. Examples include well integrity and process safety.

#### Reduce extent

Standards in this category relate to controls that limit how far a developing deviation progresses. Where prevent initiation has not been fully effective and a deviation is underway, these standards ensure that barriers are in place to contain the situation before it becomes a hazardous event. Examples include Fire and gas detection and emergency shutdown.

#### Prevent escalation

Standards in this category relate to controls that stop a hazardous event from developing into a major accident. Where a hazardous condition already exists, these standards ensure that barriers are in place to prevent it from worsening. Examples include active fire protection and passive fire protection

#### Emergency preparedness

Standards in this category relate to controls that ensure an effective response if a major accident occurs. These are the last line of defense, the barriers that protect personnel and manage the consequences of a major event. Examples include evacuation systems and emergency response procedures.
