##	Barriers

Barriers are measures intended to identify conditions that may lead to failure, hazard and accident situations, prevent an actual sequence of events occurring or developing, influence a sequence of events in a deliberate way, or limit damage and/or loss. Barrier are sometimes referred to as Safety and Environmental Critical Barriers (SECBs).

###	What is a barrier function?  

A barrier function is the task or role of a barrier. Each barrier function is implemented through one or more barrier elements, also known as Safety and Environmental Critical Elements (SECEs) — the physical or logical components that carry out the function. For example, a barrier function of "prevent overpressure" might be implemented through a combination of pressure transmitters, high-pressure trip logic, and pressure safety valves. 

###	What is a barrier element?

A barrier element is a technical, operational or organizational measure involved in the realization of a barrier function.

While a barrier function is the purpose or role the barrier is meant to perform, such as prevent, control, or mitigate an unwanted event. A barrier element is the actual component or measure that helps deliver that function, such as equipment, a device, or a procedural step.

The function is “what it does,” while the element is “what provides it”. For example, preventing leakage is a barrier function, while a valve, seal, or operating procedure can be a barrier element contributing to that function.

##	How barrier functions relate to areas and hazards  

Barrier functions are associated with both the area they protect and the major accident hazards they relate to. This means a single barrier function can appear in multiple views within the BSP — by area, by hazard, or by performance standard. 

##	Types of barrier functions:

Barrier functions can be technological (e.g. a control system trip), physical (e.g. a valve), or operational (e.g. a procedure or competency requirement). 

:::note
Operational barriers — such as control of work processes, risk assessments, and training requirements — are represented separately in the BSP but are part of the same overall barrier model.
:::

## Types of barrier elements:

Technical - The equipment or system that helps achieve the barrier function, such as hardware, machinery, or safety systems

Organisational – Personnel with defined roles, responsibilities, and competence needed to make the barrier work.

Operational – The action or activity those people must carry out to realise the barrier function.

|For example:||
|---|---|
|Barrier function:|Time to safely evacuate personnel|
|Technial barrier element:| Capacity of the means of evacuation e.g. alarm noise level|
|Organisational barrier elements:| Specific competence required for personnel e.g. participation frequency in safety drills|
|Operational barrier element:| Criteria for warning/informing e.g. time requirements|

##	Barrier Hierarchy:

The Eigen Barrier Status Panel will often use the following barrier hierarchy to display information visually.

1.	Area – The protected area being viewed.
2.	Barrier Function – The high level protective objective associated with a specific hazard.
3.	Any Barrier Sub-Functions - This is a sub-function of the barrier function above it, representing a more specific protective activity that contributes to preventing the hazard.
4.	Barrier Element - This is the barrier element, the specific operational control that implements the sub-function.
